    ADR 0009: Streamed speech resumes at text-segment boundaries¶

    Status: Accepted.

    Context¶

    Clients read long replies aloud over unreliable links (a watch over Wi-Fi or LTE): they request speech for a piece of text through the AbstractCore plugin's tts_stream(...) (POST /runs/{id}/voice/tts/stream on AbstractGateway) and play the audio as it arrives. When a stream breaks part-way, the client must request the rest.

    Skipping the seconds of audio already received does not resume exactly. Synthesis is not deterministic: Qwen3-TTS samples every generation (temperature 0.9, no seed), the speaker check can regenerate a whole generation (ADR 0004, backlog 056), and engines split text differently (Supertonic and Piper into sentence groups of at most 240 characters, the Qwen3-TTS clone into generations of up to 800 characters on MLX). A second synthesis of the same text has different timing, so a time offset lands a few words early or late: the listener hears words twice or misses them.

    What is exact is the text. Every segment an engine synthesizes is one independent generation of a known slice of the text, and its audio speaks exactly that slice.

    Decision¶

    • A streamed synthesis is a sequence of text segments. The segment-aware iterators (VoiceManager.speak_to_audio_segments, VoiceCloner.speak_to_audio_segments, and infer_to_audio_segments on engines that report their generations) yield SegmentStart, that segment's (audio, sample_rate) chunks, then SegmentEnd, in delivery order (abstractvoice.tts.segments). The chunk iterators are the same streams without the markers.
    • A segment is the engine's own unit of generation: a base engine's sentence group, one Qwen3-TTS clone generation (a regenerated take is still that segment; only the accepted take is delivered). A native-streaming base engine, and a clone engine that does not implement infer_to_audio_segments, make the whole text segment 0. Coarser segments are allowed; a segment that does not speak exactly its text is not.
    • The voice manager locates every segment in the caller's text: text_start / text_end are code-point offsets [start, end) into the text as the caller sent it. The Markdown markers the sanitizer removes and the whitespace the segmenters collapse are mapped back (SegmentLocator); a segment that is not the next slice of the text raises.
    • tts_stream puts segment, text_start and text_end on every audio event (all 2 s slices of one segment share them) and writes one {"type": "segment_end", "segment", "text_start", "text_end"} event after a segment's last audio. done carries segments, the number of segments ended.
    • The client retry rule (hybrid): after a break, keep all the audio received; request the text from text_end of the last segment_end received (from the start when there is none); when the break fell inside a segment, also skip the new stream's first seconds, as many as were received of that unfinished segment. Offsets in the retried stream index the retried text. The seam is exact at a segment boundary and approximate (a few words) inside a segment; a piece is never replayed whole. Clients do not shrink their pieces to get finer segments: one voice per reply (ADR 0008) outranks a finer seam. The retried stream's offsets index the retried text; clients add the resume offset to map them back. Audio is played as it arrives, never held until its segment_end.
    • AbstractVoice is the authoritative producer. Hosts between the plugin and the client (AbstractRuntime, AbstractGateway) forward the text and these fields unchanged; they do not strip the text, renumber segments, or derive their own offsets.

    Consequences¶

    Positive¶

    • At a segment boundary, resume is exact for every engine, whatever it samples: no word is repeated or missed at the seam.
    • Clients need no timing model of the engine and no text matching; the offsets index their own text.
    • The fields are additive: existing clients that ignore them keep working.

    Negative¶

    • Inside a segment the seam stays approximate. On the MLX backend a clone reply of up to 800 characters is one generation (ADR 0008), so a client's 600-character piece is often one segment. Discarding its partial audio would replay up to the whole piece, so the client skips the seconds it already has instead; a sampling engine speaks the text with different timing on the retry, so that seam can land a few words early or late. Exact seams inside a generation would need finer generations, which trade against one voice per reply.
    • The sanitizer removes # to ##### headers at the start of a line, **bold** and *italic* markers. Such a marker spanning two segments (*first. second.*) leaves its closing marker in the retried text. The contract is exact for plain text; clients that resume send plain text.

    Follow-up (decision pending)¶

    • The clone engine could generate streamed requests in sentence groups (about 240 characters, the base engines' bound) instead of one generation up to 800 characters. Segment-only resume would then be exact per sentence group and the approximate half of the hybrid rule would go away. ADR 0008's "one voice per reply" is already spent at the piece level, because clients send ~600-character pieces that are separate generations. The trade is more generation boundaries per piece (each one a chance of voice drift, each opening speaker-checked). This is the operator's decision (backlog 0066).

    Neutral¶

    • Base and clone engines keep their own segmentation; the contract reports it rather than imposing one.

    Enforcement¶

    • A new engine either implements infer_to_audio_segments with segments in delivery order or is treated as one segment; it never reports a segment boundary its audio does not respect.
    • A new text transform before synthesis must only delete characters (or carry its own index map into SegmentLocator); otherwise the locator raises.
    • Hosts that relay tts_stream pass the request text through unaltered and forward unknown event fields; AbstractGateway's voice stream test checks this.

    Validation¶

    • tests/test_tts_stream_segments.py: spans are exact slices of French and English text with punctuation, numbers and emoji; Markdown markers map back; the remainder after any segment speaks exactly the rest; base, native and clone paths mark segments in delivery order (through a speaker-check regeneration and with the check off); a cancelled segment never ends; the plugin's lines carry the fields on every slice; resuming from the last segment_end, after every line of a stream in turn, loses and repeats nothing at the boundary. The seconds skip inside the unfinished segment is tested in the clients (abstractassistant AbstractGatewayClient).
    • tests/test_voice_cloner_engine_dispatch.py: an engine without infer_to_audio_segments is one segment.
    • AbstractGateway tests/test_voice_stream_segments.py: the text reaches the engine as sent and the fields reach the client unchanged.
